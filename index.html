Pollutants Monitored

NO₂
Nitrogen Dioxide
Primarily emitted from combustion processes including vehicle engines and industrial facilities. Irritates the lungs and lowers resistance to respiratory infections. Measured in mol/m².
CO
Carbon Monoxide
An important atmospheric trace gas for understanding tropospheric chemistry. Main sources include combustion of fossil fuels, biomass burning, and atmospheric oxidation of methane.
SO₂
Sulphur Dioxide
Released primarily from burning fossil fuels containing sulfur. Adversely affects human health and air quality, and contributes to acid rain damaging ecosystems and buildings.

Study Overview

🛰️
Satellite Data
Data collected using the TROPOMI instrument aboard the Copernicus Sentinel-5P satellite, launched in 2017. Provides daily global coverage at 7×3.5 km spatial resolution using Differential Optical Absorption Spectroscopy (DOAS).
🗺️
Study Area
Ernakulam district, Kerala — the industrial capital of the state with the third-highest population. Known for dense population, bustling vehicular traffic, and numerous industries including the city of Kochi.
⚙️
Methodology
TROPOMI data accessed via Google Earth Engine and Copernicus Open Access Hub. Processed using GEE JavaScript API with spatial filtering, temporal aggregation, and IDW interpolation in QGIS for ground-based data mapping.
📊
Comparison
Ground-based monitoring data from Kerala Pollution Control Board validated against TROPOMI satellite observations. Analysis covers three distinct periods — pre-COVID (2019), COVID lockdown (2020), and post-COVID (2023).
2019
Period 01
Pre-COVID Baseline
Establishes baseline pollution levels under normal urban and industrial activity in Ernakulam district. High NO₂ near traffic corridors and industrial zones.
2020
Period 02
COVID Lockdown
Significant reduction in pollutant concentrations observed across all three gases due to restricted movement and reduced industrial and transportation activity.
2023
Period 03
Post-COVID Recovery
Gradual rebound in pollution levels as economic activity resumed. Comparison reveals lasting changes in emission patterns and identifies persistent pollution hotspots.
